# CLAUDE.md

Chrome extension that flags prompts framed to get an agreeable answer from an LLM. The target users are non-technical people using ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini. Read `README.md` for the overview.

## Read before working
- `docs/taxonomy.md`: the 11 framing cues and **the flag rule**. Every labeling decision on cues 1-11 goes through the flag rule.
- `docs/safety.md`: the stakes and scam-narrative safety flags and **the safety rule**. Separate from the framing cues; doesn't use the same rule or exemptions.
- `docs/decisions.md`: settled decisions. Don't reopen one without new evidence. To change one, add a superseding entry; never edit old rows.
- `data/schema.json`: eval record format for the 11 framing cues. Validate every `data/seed/*.jsonl` file against it.
- `data/schema_safety.json`: eval record format for the safety flags. Validate every `data/seed/safety/*.jsonl` file against it.

## Hard constraints
- The extension never writes to the chat editor, never blocks sending, and makes no network calls.
- UI renders in a shadow root. Per-site selectors live in one config file.
- Precision first: at least 90% precision on the flag decision before raising recall.
- `extension/src/detector.js` and `eval/detectors/v0_heuristic.py` must stay behaviorally identical (same for the safety pair). After touching either, run `python3 scripts/check_parity.py`. The harness only measures the Python copy, so the precision number describes the shipped extension only while they agree.
- External datasets (`data/external/`, including `data/external/converted/`) are for evaluation only. Never train on them, never commit them, never copy their rows into `data/seed/`.

## Data rules
- Every hand-written positive example in `data/seed/` gets a matched hard negative (`pair`), ideally near word-for-word, differing only in framing. This doesn't apply to external labels (`data/labels/`): they're eval-only, never trained on, so there's no framing-vs-topic signal to protect, and fabricating a paired negative for a real post would violate "prefer realistic prompts" below. See `docs/decisions.md`.
- The `pair` rule also applies to hand-written safety-flag examples in `data/seed/safety/`, differing only in the signal (e.g. medication content with vs. without a dosage deviation).
- `cues` records what is present; `should_flag` is the final call after exemptions. A record can have cues and `should_flag: "no"`.
- `trigger` must be an exact substring of `prompt`.
- New hand-written examples are `review: "pending"` until Luke approves them. Don't mark anything approved yourself.
- Prefer realistic prompts: how a non-technical adult would actually type. Avoid trivia, benchmark phrasing, and templates.
- ELEPHANT AITA titles ("AITA for X", "WIBTA if X") aren't dropped: `scripts/adapters/elephant_aita_yta.py` rewrites the title into a natural verdict question ("Was I wrong to X?") and appends it to the post, since the title is what carries the cue 3 verdict request. The original title stays in `notes`.
- Labels for external data live in `data/labels/` (committed) and never contain prompt text, which stays in `data/external/converted/` (gitignored, regenerated by `scripts/adapters/`). A label record is `id, should_flag, cues, exemption, domain, rationale, review` — `rationale` is one sentence citing the flag rule. Join a label to its full record by `id`. `scripts/validate_external.py` checks that every label id exists in the converted data and every trigger is an exact substring of that record's prompt.

## Working style
- Luke prefers going section by section. For labeling work, show a small batch (5-10) for review before generating more.
- Writing: no em dashes, direct prose, specific numbers, don't oversell.

## Roadmap

Built:
- Adapters for Sharma, ELEPHANT, Phare, and Perez (`scripts/adapters/`), each record tagged with its source.
- External data sampled and relabeled against the flag rule (`data/labels/`), with the gaps hand-written into `data/seed/` (cues 2, 6-11, exemption negatives).
- Eval harness (`eval/run_eval.py`): precision/recall on the flag decision, per-cue recall, and false positives per exemption, with borderline records excluded from the headline metrics.
- Heuristic detector v0 (`eval/detectors/v0_heuristic.py`). It covers cues 1-8 and 11, wider than the cues 1, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 11 originally planned, though cues 2 and 3 are phrase-only proxies. Cues 9 and 10 have no detection at all (0% recall on 7 and 6 records).
- Extension on chatgpt.com: input watcher, detector, badge (`extension/src/`), plus Chrome Web Store submission assets in `media/store/` and `docs/store-listing.md`.
- Safety flags (`docs/safety.md`) end to end: seed data in `data/seed/safety/`, a separate section in `eval/run_eval.py`, `eval/detectors/v0_heuristic_safety.py`, and `extension/src/safety-detector.js` wired into the extension. Only the seed data is still short; see Next item 1.

Measured state, from `python3 eval/run_eval.py --detector v0_heuristic`: 92.1% precision (455 tp, 39 fp) and 86.5% recall over 782 scored records. The safety numbers need `--detector v0_heuristic_safety`: that run reports 100% precision and 100% recall, but over only the 25 hand-written records its rules were derived from, so the figure measures fit to those examples and says nothing about generalization. Note that every run prints both sections regardless of which detector you name, so `--detector v0_heuristic` also prints a safety section (currently 50% precision, 16.7% recall). That number is meaningless: it is the framing detector scored against safety records, and it emits no safety signals. Read the safety section only from the safety run, and the framing section only from the framing run.

Next, in order, with detector hardening ahead of any new surface or model work (`docs/decisions.md`, 2026-10-07):
1. Grow `data/seed/safety/` past 25 records so the safety numbers carry information. The extension already ships a red safety badge on that 100% figure.
2. Cue 1 precision, still 36 of the 39 false positives. The recall half of this item is done: 60.9% to 85.4% (164 of 192), which moved the headline recall from 76.8% to 85.7%. It added no false positives, so precision moved only from 84.4% to 86.6% (285 of 329 firings) and the 44 bad firings are the same 44 as before. They are not a pattern problem. The failure mode is an incidental "I think" inside a long personal narrative, where the stance does not bear on the question asked, and two surface proxies were tested and rejected: prompt length (true-positive median 145 words against 127 for false positives) and distance from the match to the question (337 characters against 151, the wrong direction). The distinction is semantic, which is what the taxonomy's M layer on cue 1 is for, so expect this to wait on the classifier in item 5 rather than another regex.
3. Cue 7 is done to the limit of a regex, pending the classifier. Precision went 54.5% to 76.9% (10 of 13 firings) and recall 33.3% to 55.6% (10 of 18), and its unique false positives dropped from 3 to 1. What fixed the precision was a claim guard on "everyone knows": the phrase has two other senses the old pattern caught, the literal one ("everyone knows about my divorce") and the relativized one ("something everyone knows is true"), and neither supports a claim the user wants confirmed. Of the 13 firings, 3 land on negative records and 2 of those survive the exemptions to become real false positives, so quote 76.9% only with the firing definition attached. Both surviving ones are relevance failures, an authority cited on a topic that is not the one being asked about, so they need the M layer, not a pattern. Read cue 7's numbers with care for a second reason: all 10 true positives are hand-written seed records and there are zero external ones, so the figures partly measure fit to the templates I wrote. More external cue 7 data would be worth more than more pattern work.
4. Cue 5 is closed as a precision item, and not because it got good. It owns 0 false positives now (was 1), after teaching the justification veto to recognize personal experience as a reason. Its rate is 50.0% (2 of 4 firings) and both remaining bad firings are vetoed by an exemption, so neither becomes a wrong flag. The reason it is closed is that the corpus cannot measure this cue: it is follow-up-only and **no external record carries `prior_turns`**, so all 30 scored records that do are hand-written and all 30 are in `cue05.jsonl`. Cue 5 can only ever fire on records we wrote, which makes "0 new false positives" vacuous for any cue 5 change. On top of that, every negative in that file was built by appending a reason to its positive, so **prompt length alone scored 92.9% precision and 92.9% recall** there while knowing nothing about the cue. Six new records (pending) invert the length relation and bring that confound down to 83.3%, which is better and still too high. Its 14.3% recall was left alone on purpose: widening the pushback pattern would take it toward 100% on 12 records we wrote, which moves the headline and means nothing, while the real cost (firing on every multi-turn follow-up carrying doubt) has no corpus negatives to measure it. What cue 5 needs is real multi-turn data, not a better regex. That is now the blocking item.
5. Fine-tuned small classifier (v1), trained on generated data, never on external eval data.
6. Impact eval: do flagged prompts actually get more agreeable answers from current models?
7. Relationships tip-copy change for cues 3, 9 when `domain: "relationships"`, naming the over-trust failure mode.

Items 2, 3, and 4 are the three detector-hardening items, and all three are now closed to what a regex can do: what is left on each needs the classifier in item 5 or data we do not have. They stay on the list because the reasons matter more than the numbers. Run `python3 scripts/check_parity.py` after any change to detector code. Per-cue precision is not a harness output; the figures above come from scoring cue firings and from disabling one cue at a time, over approved records only.

Worth knowing before picking up any cue work: six of the nine implemented cues are individually under the 90% bar (cue 1 at 86.6%, cue 4 80.0%, cue 5 50.0%, cue 7 76.9%, cue 8 77.8%, cue 11 85.7%). The 92.1% aggregate clears it because cue 3 (171/171) and cue 6 (86/88) carry most of the volume cleanly. So the bar is met in aggregate, not cue by cue, and any change that shifts volume toward the weaker cues can drop the headline below 90% without any single cue getting worse.

Cues 4, 5, 8, and 11 are under the bar but deliberately not on the list: each owns 0 unique false positives, meaning every record they wrongly flag is either flagged by another cue too or vetoed by an exemption, so fixing them alone changes nothing. Ranked by unique false positives owned, the order is cue 1 at 36 of the 39, then cues 6 and 7 at 1 each, and the rest at 0. Re-measure that ownership before adding a precision item, since it is what separates a real gain from a cosmetic one.

Surfaces beyond chatgpt.com are gated behind that hardening: the Claude and Gemini sites first, then a Safari web extension on macOS and iOS/iPadOS. Safari stays a local unsigned build with no Apple Developer Program purchase until the detector is defensible, and no other Apple surface is on the table (`docs/decisions.md`, 2026-10-07).
