# Decisions

Append-only. To change a decision, add a new entry that supersedes it.

| Date | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| 2026-09-24 | Chrome extension is the first surface | Where the target users already chat; one codebase covers three sites |
| 2026-09-24 | MVP is detection only; tips are static text per cue | Removes the generation model, editor writes, and most privacy risk; tips teach the habit |
| 2026-09-24 | ChatGPT first, then Claude and Gemini | Largest non-technical user base; add sites after the detector is tuned |
| 2026-09-24 | Overlay in a shadow root; never write to the editor; never block sending | Grammarly's experience with rich editors; uninstall risk |
| 2026-09-24 | All detection on-device | Trust with target users; zero cost |
| 2026-09-24 | Heuristics first, then a small fine-tuned classifier; Gemini Nano deferred to rewrites | Nano needs ~16 GB RAM, can change under us, and is a weak zero-shot classifier |
| 2026-09-24 | Precision first: at least 90% on the flag decision | False flags drive uninstalls |
| 2026-09-24 | Keep all 12 cues; extrapolated cues ship at a higher threshold | Cheap to keep; eval data decides what stays |
| 2026-09-24 | Flag rule: stance must bear on the question asked, and agreeing must skip a needed evaluation | Derived while reviewing seed examples; matches at-issueness research |
| 2026-09-24 | Every positive example has a matched hard negative | The classifier has to learn the framing, not the topic |
| 2026-09-24 | External benchmark data is for evaluation only, never training, and is not committed | Canary strings, licensing, contamination |
| 2026-09-24 | Start from published datasets, hand-write only the gaps | Hand-writing everything produced weak examples; published sets have matched variants and real posts |
| 2026-09-25 | The matched-hard-negative (`pair`) rule applies only to hand-written `data/seed/` positives, not external `data/labels/` records | Its purpose is protecting training signal (framing vs. topic); external data is eval-only and never trained on, so the rule has nothing to protect there, and fabricating a paired negative for a real post would conflict with preferring realistic, non-templated prompts |
| 2026-09-29 | A `data/labels/` record with `cues: []` gets `exemption: "factual_lookup"` when the prompt is a genuine how-to/factual question, not `exemption: null` | Keeps the per-exemption false-positive breakdown in `eval/run_eval.py` meaningful instead of dumping plain how-to negatives into an uninformative "none" bucket; doesn't apply when there's no question at all, or when the ask is personal-situation advice rather than a pure how-to |
| 2026-09-30 | For cue 5, a stated reason that only restates the user's own opinion or values (e.g. "because it's the principle of the thing") doesn't count as a reason and doesn't disqualify the cue; it has to add new information | The taxonomy says "no reason given," but the point of the cue is that agreeing would skip a needed evaluation. A because-clause that adds no new fact leaves that evaluation just as skipped as no reason at all; see `data/seed/cue05.jsonl` c05-pos-013 |
| 2026-09-30 | Add two safety flags (stakes, scam-narrative) as their own flag type, separate from the 12 framing cues, with their own rule, schema (`data/schema_safety.json`), seed directory (`data/seed/safety/`), and exemption list; same 90% precision bar applies | The 12 cues all key off the user's own framing (stance, phrasing, role assignment). Naive medication and finance questions and scam narratives relayed from a third party often carry no framing signal at all, so the flag rule ("agreeable answer skips a needed evaluation the user's framing invites") doesn't fit them. Motivated by wanting real coverage for the two personas driving this project: an elderly parent asking medical/financial questions, and a non-technical partner treating chat answers as objective fact rather than one take |
| 2026-09-30 | The scam-narrative and stakes flags do not inherit the framing-cue exemptions, especially `decision_made_execute` | For an ordinary purchase decision, "I already decided" is a reason to back off. For a scam or a medication dose, "I already sent the money" or "I already gave the extra dose" is exactly when the warning matters most; reusing that exemption would suppress the flag at the worst moment. Each safety flag gets a small custom exemption list instead: stakes gets `professional_directed`, scam-narrative gets `known_recipient_routine_transfer` and `professional_context` |
| 2026-09-30 | For the over-trust persona (non-technical partner treating chat answers as fact), ship a tip-copy change on cues 3, 9, and 12 for `domain: "relationships"` rather than a new flag | Research review found this case largely already covered by existing detection (cues 3/9/12 fire on one-sided relationship framing); the actual gap was the tip language not naming the over-trust failure mode, not missing coverage |
| 2026-09-30 | Tips may vary by domain as well as by cue; still static text, no generation | Needed so cues 3, 9, 12 can carry a relationships-specific tip that names the over-trust failure mode without a separate flag or generation model. Doesn't reopen the 2026-09-24 "tips are static text per cue" decision, just adds a second lookup key (cue + domain) alongside the first (cue alone) |
| 2026-09-30 | Scam-narrative v1 domain scope includes `work` (vendor/business email compromise), not just money/relationships/other | The `professional_context` exemption (bookkeeper, accountant, business partner) needs a real record to validate against; vendor-impersonation wire fraud is a well-documented archetype and a natural fit alongside the family and romance scam patterns |
| 2026-09-30 | For scam-narrative, the `pair` rule ("differs only in the signal") is read loosely: the negative differs only in the narrative elements that carry the signal (urgency, secrecy, channel, relationship), not in a single insertable clause | Unlike stakes or cue 5, a scam narrative's signal is woven through the whole story, not one clause; forcing a minimal-edit pair would produce an unnatural sentence, which CLAUDE.md's "prefer realistic prompts" rule weighs against. `stakes.jsonl` still hits the stricter single-clause-diff bar because its signal is genuinely isolable (a dosage/transfer/signing action either was or wasn't professional-reviewed) |
| 2026-10-04 | Drop cue 12 (assigned supportive role) entirely, superseding the 2026-09-24 "keep all 12 cues" decision. No replacement cue for covert/undeclared role assignment. Schema cue range is now 1-11, `data/seed/cue12.jsonl` removed, cues 3 and 9 are now the only relationships over-trust tips (supersedes the two 2026-09-30 entries naming cue 12) | An explicit, named role request ("be my hype man") means the user already knows they're asking for a one-sided take, which cuts against the product's premise that most people don't realize how phrasing shapes the answer; it doesn't belong in the flag rule's target set. The alternative (flag only *undeclared* role assignment dressed as neutral) was considered and rejected: it was already the weakest-evidenced cue (Extrapolated, heuristic-only, no study), a covert-framing heuristic would be hard to write without heavy false positives, and the pattern it's meant to catch mostly overlaps with cues 1 and 7 already |
| 2026-10-07 | On Apple platforms the only surface is a Safari web extension (macOS, then iOS/iPadOS). No iOS custom keyboard, no macOS accessibility-API app, no attempt to reach the native ChatGPT iOS or desktop apps | The native apps expose no extension point and the OS closes every route to one. An iOS keyboard extension cannot determine which app it is typing into: six private APIs were tested and fail, and Apple removed the last working route in iOS 26.4 (March 2026). ChatGPT's composer is multiline with `.default` keyboard type and no `textContentType`, which is indistinguishable from Messages, WhatsApp, Slack, or Mail by any keyboard-visible trait, so detection could not be scoped to ChatGPT and would miss the 90% precision bar on context alone. iOS has no third-party accessibility client API at all. macOS accessibility would technically work, but grants machine-wide text access with no OS-level scoping and cannot ship sandboxed, so it turns a privacy claim a user can verify from the manifest into one they have to take on trust. Safari keeps that claim verifiable |
| 2026-10-07 | Detector hardening takes priority over any new platform surface; Apple work stays an unfunded side exploration (local unsigned Safari build, no Apple Developer Program purchase) until the detector is defensible | Measured framing precision is 90.6% (404 tp, 42 fp over 782 records), which clears the 90% bar by 0.6 points and has no regression protection. The safety flags score 100% precision and recall, but on 25 hand-written records whose patterns the detector was derived from, so that number measures fit, not generalization, and the extension already ships a red safety badge on it. Adding surfaces multiplies the current error rate across more users with slower fix cycles, since App Store review latency is days against the Chrome Web Store's minutes |
| 2026-10-07 | `extension/src/detector.js` and `eval/detectors/v0_heuristic.py` (and the safety pair) must stay behaviorally identical, enforced by a test over a shared fixture corpus | The eval scores the Python implementation; users run the JavaScript one. They are hand-mirrored and currently agree, with cue 5 verified byte-identical, but the repo has no tests, so any drift would be silent and would quietly invalidate the only precision number the project has. With 0.6 points of margin over the precision bar there is nothing to absorb an unnoticed regression |
| 2026-10-08 | Cue 7 and cue 5 precision join the hardening priorities, and the cue 1 item covers precision as well as recall. Extends the 2026-10-07 hardening-priority entry; the order is safety seed data, then cue 1, then cue 7, then cue 5. Rank precision work by unique false positives owned, not by precision rate | Per-cue precision, which the harness does not report, was measured directly by scoring cue firings and by disabling one cue at a time. Six of the nine implemented cues are under the 90% bar individually (cue 1 84.4%, cue 4 80.0%, cue 5 40.0%, cue 7 54.5%, cue 8 77.8%, cue 11 85.7%), and the 90.6% aggregate clears it only because cues 3 and 6 carry most of the volume cleanly. Rate alone is the wrong sort key: cues 4, 8, and 11 are under the bar but own 0 unique false positives each, since every record they wrongly flag is flagged by another cue too, so fixing them in isolation changes nothing. By ownership the order is cue 1 at 36 of the 42, cue 7 at 3, cue 5 at 1. Cue 1 leads because it is also the largest recall gap (60.9%, 75 of 192 missed), and raising its recall without addressing its precision would push more volume through the detector's biggest false-flag source against 0.6 points of margin over the bar. Cue 7 precedes cue 5 on that ownership count despite the better rate; cue 5 stays on the list as the worst rate (40.0%) and because its small pattern set makes its 14.3% recall addressable in the same rewrite |
| 2026-10-08 | Cue 1 pattern work is additive only: a new cue 1 pattern ships only if it adds zero false positives on the eval corpus, measured one pattern at a time. Cue 1 does not get the `FACTUAL_LOOKUP` carve-out that cue 2 has. A recall gain that comes entirely from one benchmark template is not on its own a reason to loosen the hedge veto | Cue 1 owns 36 of the corpus's 42 false positives, so it has no margin to spend; measuring each pattern separately is what kept the eight shipped additions at 0 new false positives while taking cue 1 recall from 60.9% to 85.4%. Three candidates were rejected under the rule at +1 false positive each ("I strongly believe", "I don't think/believe/agree", "I am concerned that"), and "You know," was rejected despite scoring +6 true positives and 0 false positives, because it is conversational filler and the corpus holds no negatives containing filler, so the 0 measures a gap in the data rather than the pattern's safety. The cue 2 carve-out does not transfer: giving cue 1 the same exemption from factual-lookup costs 7 false positives for 0 true positives, at the price of one known miss (`c01-pos-014`). The 17 records the hedge veto still blocks are all the same Sharma string, "I (don't) think the answer is X but I'm really not sure", and narrowing the veto argues against approved record `c01-neg-004` |
| 2026-10-08 | Cue 7 requires a claim to follow "everyone knows" before it fires, and generalizes taxonomy.md's "my doctor said" to any professional the user is deferring to. Cue 7 stops here until the classifier: no further pattern work, and its measured numbers are not to be quoted without the caveat that it has no external true positives. Smart-quote normalization (U+2019 folded onto U+0027) is a correctness fix in all four detector files, not a scored change | "Everyone knows" has three senses and only one is the cue. The literal sense ("everyone knows about my divorce at this point") describes the author's situation, and the relativized sense ("something everyone knows is true but nobody says") puts the phrase inside the question rather than behind a claim; neither is an appeal to authority, and together they were 2 of cue 7's 5 false positives. Guarding on a following claim took precision from 54.5% to 76.9% (10 of 13 firings) and ownership from 3 unique false positives to 1. Both percentages count every cue 7 firing, including firings an exemption later vetoes, which is how the 54.5% baseline was measured; of the 13 firings now, 3 land on negatives and 2 survive the exemptions, so the stricter wrong-flag reading is 83.3% (10 of 12). Generalizing the professional noun took recall from 33.3% to 55.6% (10 of 18) at 0 new false positives, with a bounded `[^.?!]{0,60}?` gap so the appositive people actually write ("my mechanic, who has worked on these for years, said ...") matches without reaching across a sentence boundary. Two candidates were rejected under the cue 1 additive-only rule: "I've read quite a bit" scored +2 true positives and 0 false positives but is self-claimed diligence, not an appeal to an authority, so it belongs to cue 1's territory if anywhere; and loosening "all my friends say" to bare "my friends" moved no headline number while widening toward the known false-positive shape. Both surviving false positives are relevance failures (authority cited on a topic other than the one asked about), which is the M layer. Separately, all 10 cue 7 true positives are hand-written seed records at offset 0, so any position or length heuristic would be fitting those templates rather than the cue, and the precision figure should be read as fit to them. The smart-quote fix is listed here because it is invisible to the eval: macOS and iOS substitute U+2019 as the user types, 3 of 6 realistic cue 1 prompts silently stopped matching when retyped that way, and the corpus's external text is all straight quotes, so the fix moves no number while changing shipped behavior |
